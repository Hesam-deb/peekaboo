Unlock your Linux laptop by looking at it.

Infrared face unlock for the lock screen, login, sudo and system prompts. A little friend on your screen looks back at you while it works.

Install Peekaboo Read the source

Looking for you.

Eight friends to choose from

On your laptop they mirror how you turn your head and when you blink. Here they follow your pointer, drawn by the same code the GNOME extension uses. Pick one to use it above too.

It only believes its own light

The infrared emitter flickers. Peekaboo subtracts the unlit frames from the lit one, so what is left is light it sent out and got back. It works in the dark and is blind to faces on screens.

What gets recognisedOnly you

A head turn for what matters

sudo and system prompts ask for a small turn. It shows that you are not a flat picture, and that you mean it.

No pictures kept

Only numbers are stored, encrypted with a key sealed to the TPM, behind a sandboxed service.

Your password always works

In a terminal you can simply type it while the camera looks. Five failures, a reboot or 48 hours ask for it.

Only where you want it

Switch it on separately for the lock screen, login screen, terminal and system prompts.

Two cameras to register

The colour camera watches along and both must agree it is one face. Only infrared is stored or used to unlock.

At the laptop only

Not over ssh, not with the lid closed, and only for the person whose session is on the screen.

Set up in the app, or the terminal

Move your head to complete the circle and you are registered. Loosening a setting asks for your password. Tightening one does not.

Read this before relying on it

Peekaboo is a convenience. It does not replace a strong password.

Full security notes and error rates

  • There is no depth sensor. At the lock and login screens no head turn is asked by default, so a good infrared-reflective print of your face might pass. You can turn it on there.
  • Curved prints and masks are not defended against, and no spoofing with physical artefacts has been tested.
  • Identical twins and close relatives may match.
  • A face cannot unlock the GNOME keyring.
  • Root can do anything, including read templates while the key is loaded.
  • It was developed on ASUS Zenbooks with a Shinetech 3277:0055 camera. Other cameras are simulated in the tests.

Install

For Debian 13 and Ubuntu 24.10 or newer, on amd64 or arm64, with an infrared camera. Animations need GNOME 48 to 50.

Laptops without infrared are not supported, because a colour camera cannot tell a face from a photo. Neither are MIPI (IPU6/IPU7) infrared sensors that Linux does not expose.

Download the latest .deb

Build from source, or try Fedora and Arch (untested there)

  1. Install the package you downloaded

    sudo apt install ./peekaboo_*.deb

  2. Check your laptop and register

    peekaboo doctor        # does everything check out?
peekaboo gui           # set up your face, try it
peekaboo animation on  # GNOME; log out and in once

  3. Switch it on

    Keep a root shell open until you know you can still get in.

    sudo pam-auth-update --enable peekaboo peekaboo-password

    To undo it, use --disable. sudo apt remove peekaboo takes everything out.
